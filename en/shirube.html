shirube (道標, "signpost")
From the job you want to a timetable through graduation

Enter a job title, and an LLM works out the skills it requires, embedding search over the syllabi gathers candidate courses, an LLM rereads the candidates and picks courses with a reason for each (RAG with LLM reranking), and the courses are laid out in a semester-by-semester timetable. I designed it alone and launched it in production. Before starting on improvements, I built an evaluation framework that measures accuracy, variation and cost, and that is what decides whether a change gets adopted.

02Background and goals

When choosing courses, reading the syllabi one by one still doesn't tell you where a course will help in your future job, and you have to check timetable conflicts yourself. On top of that, jobs and courses use different vocabulary ("data scientist" vs. "probability and statistics", for example), so keyword search can't connect them.

shirube works backward from the job you want: it selects courses based on what their syllabi actually say, attaches the reason for each choice, and lays them out in a semester-by-semester timetable through graduation. The first target is my own department; I ingested 530 pages of public syllabi.

03Key features

  • Selects the courses central to the job (core courses) and the courses they build on (foundation courses), each with a reason and a link to the original syllabus page.
  • Lays them out over the remaining semesters, respecting the year each course is offered to, the semester it runs in, period conflicts, and the 16-credit-per-semester cap. Viewable as a route map and as a weekly timetable.
  • Generation takes 10–30 seconds, so it runs as an async job; while it runs, the page shows which of the four stages it has reached and the expected wait. It keeps going even if you close the page.
  • For an ambiguous job title, the screen shows how it was interpreted. Courses you've already taken are remembered and excluded from the candidates. Works at phone width, with WCAG 2.2 AA as the target.

Technical highlights

  1. Instead of an agent, a fixed four-stage pipeline: Interpret (LLM), Retrieve (embeddings), Rerank (LLM), Schedule (algorithm). This makes it possible to count which stage dropped an expected course.
  2. Built the evaluation set (50 dev jobs, 20 holdout jobs) and the comparison tooling first, and wrote the adoption criteria in an ADR before each change, then measured.
  3. A 3-way majority vote reduced errors and variation; the added cost was recovered by narrowing the candidates, prompt (input) caching, and reusing interpretations.
  4. Built for public release: a daily limit, prepaid billing, structured output that cannot return a course outside the candidates, and external monitoring every 15 minutes.

06Prompt and context design

Both calls use LangChain structured output (json_schema, strict).

①

Interpret (gpt-5.4-mini)

Input
A role (career advisor). A rule: take jobs that the department's courses directly prepare for at face value, and read only narrowly specialized roles more broadly. The user's job title.
Output
An interpretation of the job title, an assumption (null if none), and 3–6 skills (noun phrases of up to 20 characters).
Approach
Skills are written as the names of fields taught in courses rather than product names ("how the web works and programming" rather than React), which pulls search queries toward the language of syllabi. If the model rephrases the job title without stating an assumption, code restores the original job title.
③

Rerank (gpt-5.4-nano × 3)

Input
Definitions of core and foundation courses and the order to select them in. The job, the assumption and the skills. Excerpts of the 20 candidates (overview 450 characters, learning objectives 250 characters, per-session topics 350 characters).
Output
Course names, and the core course each foundation course leads to, are restricted to an enum of the candidates. Reasons are at most 80 characters.
Approach
Excerpts are cut exactly the same way as for the judges who built the ground truth, so differences in input don't leak into the evaluation. The model may not write anything that isn't in the excerpts. The second vote is delayed by 0.5 seconds so that it hits the prompt cache.

07Technology and design decisions

Every one of these was decided from measurements or from the results of trying it.

DecisionRationale
A workflow with fixed stages, not an agentWith an agent, you can't separate out and measure where it went wrong. Each stage has its own metrics. And because LLM output is never used for tool calls or DB queries, the impact of prompt injection stops at the text on the screen
Narrow with retrieval, then have the LLM rereadAbout 20% of the top 10 retrieval results are judged irrelevant. When the LLM reads the content, it can drop them and write reasons grounded in evidence. Having it read every course would make the input 5× larger or more, and it couldn't be extended to other departments
A different model per stageFor Interpret, on 4 ambiguous job titles mini stated the assumption in 4 and nano in 1, so mini. For Rerank, over 10 jobs × 2 runs nano was on par with or better than mini at about a quarter of the cost, so nano
Search with per-skill queries (query decomposition)Most of the misses came from skills being diluted within a single query. In an interleaved comparison over 50 jobs, the irrelevant share fell from 23.0% to 15.1% (p = 0.001)
Greedy timetabling, async generationWhen an LLM builds the timetable, periods overlap. With synchronous generation, results get saved after the page has already timed out, and regenerating doubles the cost

08Challenges and solutions

01

LLM answers varied, and irrelevant courses got mixed in

Problem
With the original baseline, 23% of courses were irrelevant and the overlap between regenerations was 0.45.
Solution
Calling Rerank 3 times in parallel and keeping only courses with 2 or more votes cut the irrelevant share to 10%, but the cost rose from ¥0.81 to ¥1.57. So I narrowed the candidates from 30 to 20 (¥1.14), delayed the second vote to raise the prompt cache hit rate from 50% to 63% (¥1.01), and reused job interpretations. In interleaved runs against the original baseline, the cost stayed at ¥0.83 while the irrelevant share went from 27.6% to 21.6% and the overlap from 0.52 to 0.72.
02

A public launch risked someone running up the costs

Problem
At first, failed roadmaps didn't count toward the daily limit. During a review I realized that by mixing instructions into the job title to make generation fail on purpose, someone could spend without any limit.
Solution
Failures now count toward the limit. The output is restricted to candidate courses by an enum, and a roadmap is visible only to its owner (other users' roadmaps return 404), so the effect of injected instructions stays on the submitter's own screen. I ran 3 job titles with injected instructions and recorded that the model didn't follow them and output neither courses outside the candidates nor the API key; I rerun them every time I change the instructions or the model.

09Evaluation framework

LLM output always looks plausible, so I built this before any improvements, to decide what to adopt by the numbers rather than by feel.

  1. Evaluation set50 dev jobs, 20 holdout jobs
  2. Interleaved runsAlternate the two configurations by job and by run
  3. ComparePermutation tests and intervals. Also count which stage dropped each course
  4. ADRWrite the adoption criteria before measuring. Record rejections too
  5. HoldoutRun once, only after the change is final; look only at the means and the test

How the ground truth is built

There is no ground truth for each job, so three Claude subagents (an engineer who does hiring, a faculty member who designs the curriculum, and a third-year student thinking about their career) independently labeled the candidate courses as "central", "foundational" or "irrelevant", and I took the majority vote (LLM-as-a-Judge). That gives 151 expected courses in dev and 58 in holdout. The judges come from a different model family than the OpenAI models the app uses.

The same three judges rated the reason text on a three-level scale: specific, vague, or none (agreement on core-course reasons: Fleiss' κ 0.80). To make this cheaper to run, I also built judges with gpt-5.4-nano and mini, but on 75 holdout items their Cohen's κ was 0.54, short of the 0.6 threshold I had set in advance, so I didn't adopt them.

Holdout jobs are flagged in the evaluation set, and the experiment tooling doesn't output per-job results for them, which prevents me from accidentally looking at their contents.

Fixes to the evaluation itself

  • A/A tests showed false differences. Even between identical configurations, bootstrap intervals reported a significant difference 10% of the time (twice the nominal 5%). Switching to a per-job permutation test brought it back within 5%.
  • Results shifted with the time of the run. With the same configuration and code, runs at different times moved the irrelevant share by about 9 points. Interleaving the two configurations by job and by run removed the time-of-run drift from the difference.
  • I was overfitting to the dev set. A retrieval improvement raised candidate recall by 6.4 points on the 30 jobs I had looked at while deciding on the change, but lowered it by 1.2 points on the 20 jobs added later. So I decided to keep a separate holdout set aside.

Known gaps

  • The ground truth is a majority vote of three LLM judges; agreement with human judgments hasn't been measured yet.
  • No students have used it yet, so I haven't verified that it's actually useful.
  • Core-course precision is low, at 37–42%, because I prioritized coverage and made core-course selection broader.
  • Required courses, graduation requirements and prerequisite ordering aren't checked, and it covers only one department.

Next steps

  • Have students and faculty in the department label a subset, and measure agreement (κ) with the Claude judges.
  • Of the 151 expected courses, 13% are dropped at Rerank, 12% at Interpret and 3% at Retrieve. Next I'll fix the variation in Interpret, but continuing to tune on the same 50 jobs would overfit, so I'll add unseen jobs before verifying.
  • Have students use it during course registration (March–April), and look at how often they regenerate and which courses they mark as already taken, together with the build records.
  • Add required courses and graduation requirements, and switch timetabling to integer programming. First, measure how many roadmaps the greedy approach can't make meet the requirements.

Outlook

  • Expand to other departments, faculties and universities. Right now it covers only my own department. I chose to narrow the candidates with retrieval before the LLM rereads them because having it read every course would make the input 5× larger or more and rule out expansion; it is built on the assumption that it will expand.
  • I'd also like to start a mobile app. The current web version already works at phone width, but I want to deliver it as an app too.
